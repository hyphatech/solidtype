`Solidtype_heroui.Table` is HeroUI's Table, its classes HeroUI's own
`tableVariants`, around a `<table role="grid">` moved through by the keys as
React Aria's grid is. Kobalte has no table: its rows are a list of Kobalte's,
made of the hooks Kobalte's own lists are, `Solidtype_kobalte.Selection`,
which focus a row and move between rows by the arrow keys, Home and End, Page
Up and Page Down and typing; the port moves along a row's cells and between a
cell and the cell above or below it.

Rows given by data are Solid's `For` inside `Table.Body`, as HeroUI's are its
body's `items` and a render function; columns and cells given by data are a
`For` too, as HeroUI's are its header's `columns` and `Table.Collection`.

Its rows are selected by Kobalte's list, one or many, by a press, Space or
Enter, or a `Checkbox` of `slot=`selection` in a row, and all of them by one
in the header. A column that allows sorting is pressed, or given Enter or Space, to be
sorted by. `Table.Load_more` loads more rows as the table's end scrolls into
view. In a `Table.Resizable_container`, a `Table.Column_resizer` resizes its
column. Given a `tree_column`, rows hold rows, expanded and collapsed by a
chevron or the arrow keys. Virtualization is being ported, in a stage of its
own; its example is added with it.

### Async Loading

`Table.Load_more` at the end of `Table.Body` calls `on_load_more` as it comes
within `scroll_offset` heights of the view of what scrolls the table, and
again as loading ends with it still in view; while `is_loading` it draws a row
across every column holding its `Table.Load_more_content`. It never asks while
loading, so the application keeps no guard of its own, where HeroUI's example
keeps one in a ref, since React Aria asks again while the rows load.

<Example code={ExAsyncLoadingCode} title="table_async_loading.mlx"><ExAsyncLoading.make client:visible /></Example>

### Sorting

`allows_sorting` and `id` on a `Table.Column`, and `sort_descriptor` and
`on_sort_change` on `Table.Content`, sort it; the rows' order is the
application's. `Table.Sortable_column_header` draws HeroUI's chevron for its
column's direction, which it reads from its column: HeroUI's is given it by
the column's render function.

<Example code={ExSortingCode} title="table_sorting.mlx"><ExSorting.make client:visible /></Example>

### Selection

### Expandable Rows

`tree_column` on `Table.Content`, the `id` of a column, makes the table a tree
grid: a `Table.Row` written inside another, among its cells, is drawn after it
while it is expanded, a level deeper, its tree column's cell indented by
HeroUI's styles. `expanded_keys`, or `default_expanded_keys`, and
`on_expanded_change` say which rows are expanded. A `Button` of
`slot=`chevron` in a row expands and collapses it; the arrow forward on a row
expands it, the arrow back collapses it, then reaches the row it is in.
`Table.use_row_context` and `Table.use_cell_context`, read in a cell, are
what HeroUI's cell's render function is given: whether its row holds rows and
is expanded, and whether it is in the tree column. A context is read as the
cell is drawn, and kept, so a handler reads the row it was drawn in.

### Column Resizing

`Table.Resizable_container` in place of `Table.Scroll_container` lays the
columns out at a fixed width, each as its `width`, or `default_width` until it
is resized, says: `Pixels`, a `Percent` of the table's width, or a `Fraction`
of what the others leave, never narrower than `min_width`, 75 pixels by
default, nor wider than `max_width`. A `Table.Column_resizer` in a column is
dragged, or reached by Tab from the column's header, where its arrows resize
the column by 10 pixels and Enter begins a resize the arrows go on with, which
Enter, Escape, Space, Tab or leaving it ends. `on_resize_start`, `on_resize`
and `on_resize_end` on the container are told every column's size by its
`id`.

<Example code={ExColumnResizingCode} title="table_column_resizing.mlx"><ExColumnResizing.make client:visible /></Example>
